# Content audit: First Builds for someone who wasn't there

Written October 11, 2026, on the branch `north-shore-look`, from the plan in `docs/CONTENT-PASS.md`. This is the audit only. Nothing on the site has been rewritten. The decisions Sean needs to make come first, then the findings page by page, each tied to the bar in the plan and to the file and line it points at. The last section says what became of each finding, to be filled in as pages are rewritten.

## How it was made

Every page was rendered in a headless browser (Chrome, driven over its debugging protocol by a small script, `render.js`, kept outside the repo in this chat's scratchpad) and turned into the text a reader sees: headings, body, every link with its destination, every button, folded content marked as folded, dialogs as they open, each stage screen in turn, and each speaker-notes screen. Each page was captured in its after-the-event state (what any visitor sees today) and at eight moments on the event day (09:30, then one moment inside each part). The Play windows were captured with each starter open and with the code pane shown, the six starters themselves were captured directly, the panel that appears after a successful copy was captured, and the first forty lines of each Apps Script were included as a reader sees them in the editor.

Seventeen cold readers then read the packets. Each got only a persona and one page, and nothing else: not this plan, not the repo, not the other pages. Two personas:

- **The cold educator.** A teacher, coach, or school leader who found the link on a Sunday; school Google account, Gemini, maybe ChatGPT or Claude; a district laptop where some sites and sometimes the clipboard are blocked; has never written code; twenty minutes; nobody to ask.
- **The would-be host.** A teacher-leader who wants to run the session next month over Meet for 20 to 40 colleagues; has run workshops and breakout rooms, never a hackathon; has never written code; never saw the original.

Both personas read all eight pages (Home, Three levels, Starters, Grant a wish, Sources, Hosts, Present, Speaker notes). The educator also read the six Play windows and the two scripts; the host also read the README's "Run your own session." Each reader wrote what the page is in ten seconds, what every button does as far as they can tell, every stall with a quote and a proposed rewrite, the words they didn't know, what they'd need that isn't there, and whether they could do each task.

Then I followed the instructions myself in the browser on the local preview: Play, Show the code, Run my changes, Copy for my AI from the card and from the Play window, a remix copy, Copy the script, the wish builder with empty and filled boxes, a wish from the jar, the Hosts room calculator and copy buttons, and the stage and notes controls, as far as each goes before it leaves the site. Google Sites, Apps Script, and the AI tools themselves were not exercised; what they show is marked ⚠️ VERIFY where it matters.

**Capture limits, so nothing below is blamed on them.** The renderer flattened links inside headings to plain text, so both Sources readers reported that no source title is a link. In the browser every title is a link to the source (`assets/sources.js:181`), so that finding is dropped; the "Copy link" ambiguity next to it stands. The Play dialog's embedded tool could not be read through its sandbox, so the starter transcripts stood in for it. Numbered steps showed their number twice ("1. 1**Guess**"), a rendering artifact the readers were told to ignore. The two marks (MNGAIA's and Sean's) have empty alt text on purpose, with the name beside them as text; a reader flagged it, and it is not a content finding.

**Counts.** Across the seventeen reads: 306 stalls, 45 of them rated BLOCKS by the reader who found them. They collapse into about ninety distinct findings below, because the same stall was found on several pages or by both personas. The plan's five hypotheses for the level 3 scripts were all confirmed, by both personas, on both pages that carry them.

## The short version

Ranked by how many pages and readers they stop, and by what they stop.

1. **Nothing tells a visitor the event is over and that everything works on your own.** Every page still speaks to the morning: "First, find your room," "On the day," "Live on the day," "Before Friday," "today," "the same morning as slides," "come find one of us," "Ask for help in your breakout room," "For Friday" on 22 of 45 sources, "Next month." Readers either think they missed something they can still join, or that the page isn't for them. Both personas, all eight pages.
2. **The level 3 scripts can't be followed from the site.** Both pages and both script headers leave out the place (New project, delete what's there, Save), what an ID is and where it ends, where the function menu is, what the permission screen actually says, where the Execution log is, and what the result looks like. The Doc Multiplier never states the Sheet's layout. "Read it, then allow" plus a screen that asks to "see, edit, create, and delete" your Drive files ⚠️ VERIFY means the careful reader, the one the line is written for, quits. Five BLOCKS on the Starters cards alone.
3. **The hand-off to the AI is in the wrong place, and success is never described.** Where Canvas, Preview, and artifacts are lives in the panel that appears only after a successful copy (`assets/site.js:27-31`) and in a fold at the foot of Starters, after level 3. No page says what you'll see when it worked ("the tool, with buttons, in a panel beside the chat") or what it looks like when you skipped Canvas (code and no app). "Canvas" reads as the Canvas LMS to every educator reader.
4. **The event's shared links sit on newcomer paths.** The Teams doc is the first bold instruction on Starters and the "Pick your level" step on Three levels; the gallery wall is step 6 of Grant a wish and a whole section; the Meet, the walls, and the Teams doc are the Materials row on Hosts. Every reader who met one asked whether they were allowed to write in a stranger's file. Hosts asked how to make their own, and found no answer. (Decision 1.)
5. **Hosts is a record of October 9, not a guide to running your own.** No "run it with your group" section; no roles (host, co-host, anchor); the three-tab demo has no setup; the three levels, the four steps, and the awards are named on the stage but never in the notes or on Hosts; the chat messages carry the live URL and the original Teams doc; the README path needs GitHub, git, and Node with no no-code route. (Decision 3.)
6. **Present and the speaker notes trap a cold visitor.** Nothing on screen 1 says these are the slides of a past session; "Open the site" is not labelled as the way back; "Call to order," the session clock, "Jump to now," and "Wait time" feel risky to press; after the event the notes window shows "2:00:00 to go" beside "time to move on" on every screen (`notes.html:148-154`). (Decision 2.)
7. **The site's own words are never defined where they first appear.** Starter, level, "in the chat / on your site / behind the scenes," Remix Arcade, Show & Tell versus Show & Cheer, anchor, wish, wall, kit, stage, session file, workbench, artifact, Preview, Execution log, project, ID. Every reader listed twenty or more. A one-line definition at first use on each page, or one glossary every page links to, ends this.
8. **There's no "Keep it" step.** Starters promises Monday but stops at "see what happens." Changes in the Play window vanish on Close (by design), and nothing says how to save a remixed version in each AI tool and open it Monday.
9. **Smaller but real:** the Group Maker card promises "keeps apart" that the running tool doesn't show (it's in the code, `cabinets/group-maker.html:23`); Sources dates disagree (October 7 or 8 in the lede, October 7 in the footer); a tab and a topic filter are both named "Coding and thinking" with different counts; the "Now: Show & Tell" link lands on "Pick your level"; the closing chat message is the only one without its link; "gallery wall" on Hosts goes to two places; the building screens carry a 25:00 timer inside a 57-minute build; MNGAIA · AI4MN and s.beaverson are never expanded.

What the readers found clear, and should stay: the home page's "Everything here" cards and "Show one person"; the three-level overview at the top of Three levels and the Google Sites steps; the four level 1 cards and the Play windows themselves; the wish builder's three boxes and steps 3 and 4; "The short version" and every "The habit" line on Sources; the room calculator, the ready-to-paste messages, and "Safe play" on Hosts; the stage's arrows, keys, and screen timers; the "Next:" line on every notes screen.

## Decisions for Sean, before any rewriting

Each changes what several pages say. A recommendation is given with each, and the proposed fixes below are written in branches where the decision matters.

**1. The event's shared links: Meet, the Teams doc, the walls deck.**
- A. *Keep them as the record, off the newcomer paths* (recommended). They stay in the session file and appear only on Hosts, in a folded "How the first run went" block, labelled as October 9's, with the Teams doc and walls set to view-only (as `HANDOFF.md` already says). Starters, Three levels, Grant a wish, and the stage lose them; the "pin it to the wall" section on Grant a wish becomes "keep it and show one person," and the live-session version of each page gets its link from whatever the host puts in their own session file.
- B. *Remove them from the site.* Blank them in the session file; the pages already say "coming soon" for an empty link. Cleaner, but the record of the first run goes with it.
- C. *Leave them where they are, label them.* Smallest change, and every reader still lands in a stranger's file.
Whichever: the Meet link should go now; it is a live room anyone can join.

**2. Present in the menu.** The October 11 decision kept it, like the What We Talk About site. Two of seventeen readers clicked it cold; both felt trapped and afraid to press anything.
- A. *Keep it, and fix the landing* (recommended): a one-line title-screen note ("The stage from First Builds, first run October 9, 2026. → to look through it; Back to the site to leave"), rename "Open the site" to "Back to the site," and a small "For whoever runs the session" label over the clock and timer controls.
- B. *Move it to Hosts* (Materials already links it) and the notes. The menu loses a pill; nobody stumbles in.

**3. Hosts: record, guide, or both.**
- A. *Guide first, record folded* (recommended). The page opens with "Run it with your own group": the two paths (from this site with your own Google copies and Meet; or your own copy of the site via the README), who you need and what each does, what to make before the day (with links into Three levels for the three demo tabs), what participants' accounts must allow, how the stage and notes work, then the run of show. "Before Friday" becomes "Before the day." The October 9 specifics (its Meet, Teams doc, walls, "A month in," "Next month") fold into a "How the first run went" block.
- B. *Record only.* Keep it as the day's run sheet, labelled as such, and send would-be hosts to the README. Cheaper; the host persona could not run the session from the README alone.

**4. The two scripts: Starters or Three levels.**
- A. *Keep them on Starters as a visibly different kind of card* (recommended), headed "Script · runs in Apps Script, nothing to play," with prerequisites first, every step naming its place, "Copy the script" alone on the card and "Copy for my AI" explained ("the script plus a note asking your AI to explain it, line by line, before you run it"), and the steps coming from one shared block that both Starters and Three levels fill (a `data-steps="inventory"` slot in `site.js`, the way `data-script` fills the code), so the two pages can't disagree again.
- B. *Move them to Three levels only.* Starters keeps "things you can play," level 3 becomes a card that says "Scripts live on Three levels" and links. Simpler page, one more hop for the reader who came to Starters first.

**5. Push the branch and open a pull request.** Nothing on `north-shore-look` is live; the live site still shows the old look and all of the above. The rewrite can land page by page on this branch and go up as one PR, or the look can go up first. Sean's call.

**6. Which host path the site promises (new).** The README path needs a GitHub account, a fork, and editing a JavaScript file; git and Node for the checks. The host persona rated it BLOCKS. Two paths are possible: (a) *run from this site as-is* with your own Meet, your own copies of the Google files, and chat messages you edit before pasting; (b) *your own copy of the site* for your own date, clock, links, and names. Recommended: promise both, write (a) on Hosts for the non-coder and (b) in the README with a click path and "steps 4 and 5 are checks, skip them if you edit on GitHub's site." Either way, the chat messages in `assets/session.js:86,107,155,169` should use `{url}` instead of typing the live address, or a copy of the kit sends people to inquiredu.org.

**7. The Teams doc's name (new).** Seven of seventeen readers read "Teams doc" as Microsoft Teams. Recommended: call it "the sign-up doc" everywhere (the session file's `shared` labels, the stage, the notes, Hosts, the pages), and retitle the Google Doc to match. This touches the Google file, so it is Sean's.

**8. Real names in the running tools (new).** The Group Maker says "(made-up for today)" and nothing about where names go; the Feedback Builder says "stays on this page" beside "no names." Neither tool sends anything anywhere (no network calls in either file). Recommended stance, stated inside each tool and on its card: "Names typed here stay in this browser window; nothing is saved or sent. Names pasted into an AI chat go to that company." Sean decides whether the site says real names are fine inside the tools.

**9. The "For Friday" labels on Sources (new).** 22 of 45 entries open their note with "For Friday:", plus "After Friday," "For November," "For Friday and November." Recommended: "For the session:" and "After the session:" (they live in `assets/sources-data.js` as `takeLabel`), and one sentence at the top saying what the session was. "The habit:" stays.
