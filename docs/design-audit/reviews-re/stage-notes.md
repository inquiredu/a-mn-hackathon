# The stage · Speaker notes · design review

Measurements are read off the screenshots: px at 1280 are image px; at 375 and 560 they are CSS px (half the 2x image px) unless marked "2x".

## The five that matter most

1. **P4**: The Oracle frame on screen 3 is shorter than the starter inside it. At 1280, the size the room actually sees, a line of text under "Consult the Oracle" is cut off by the frame's bottom edge. At 375 the frame ends partway through the input. This is the one screen the room interacts with live, and it looks broken.
2. **P5**: When a wait runs inside the open clock, two countdowns that look almost the same sit stacked: "1:59" above "1:59:55". The session clock is the bigger copper number, but the wait is what the room is waiting on, so the hierarchy is upside down.
3. **P9**: Text the room has to read is set at 17–22px at 1280: rung descriptions, award descriptions, the code moral, and the screen-1 paragraph. In a Meet tile that comes out at about 8–11px. Meanwhile 40–55% of most slides is empty below the content, so there is room to set it larger.
4. **P1, P2, P3**: The phone stage breaks in three ways. The part name prints on top of the clock pill on 7 of 21 screens. The wait pill covers the license and the counter. Content runs under the rail with no way to scroll, which loses the last plan row and the URL, the third award, the timer label, and the Oracle's input.
5. **N1, N2**: In the speaker notes, each screen's note uses a different structure: no label, an inline "Say,", SAY+DO, or CO-HOST+SAY. Every label is copper, as is the card's left rule, so the presenter can't pick out their own spoken lines from directions and co-host tasks at a glance.

## Region by region

### The stage

- **Rail (every screen).** It holds the author's mark and license, the part name with its length and room, the counter, and the session clock pill. That's the right content: it tells a late joiner where the session is without asking. At 1280 the counter floats unattached (P20). At 375 the part name collides with the clock (P1), and the wait pill covers the license (P2). The rail's top hairline doubles as a session progress line that fills over the two hours in Water. It's a quiet, good idea, but it doesn't change when the session runs over (P14).
- **Presenter toolbar.** The outlined buttons are uniform and 42px tall, and the arrows are ≥40px. That's comfortable. "Back to the site" uses a style that reads as disabled (P12). At 375 the toolbar becomes a panel covering the partner credit and titles (P12).
- **Screen 1, title.** Partner mark and name lead, as required. "First Builds" at ≈130px is the right anchor. The paragraph is driver instructions, too small to read in Meet, and the clock floats unaligned (P10, P9).
- **Prompt screens (2, 5, 6).** Eyebrow, copper display, serif body: the eye lands on the copper ask, then the instruction. This is the deck's strongest pattern. The body wraps early and leaves one word dangling, and the lower half sits empty (P9). Screen 8 borrows the copper display without being a chat prompt (P8).
- **Screen 3, embed.** The title and caption line up well. The frame cuts off the starter (P4), and the wait pill would cover its corner (P2).
- **Screen 4, plan.** A clean three-column list on hairlines, easy to scan. The title is jammed against the first row, and the session's shape doesn't read at a glance (P17, P21). At 375 rows take three lines each and the last rows are lost (P3).
