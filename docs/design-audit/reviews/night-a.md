# Night (dark theme): Home, Three levels, Starters · design review

Image names below drop the shared path. `home/…`, `levels/…`, and `starters/…` are the page slices. `states/…` are the interactive captures. Pixel figures are CSS px, read off the 2x phone shots by halving.

**Verdict on Night as a theme:** mostly designed, not inverted. The navy scale gives three readable surfaces (header and footer band, page, card) plus a lighter slate inset, cream text holds strong contrast, and the eyebrows and serif body read well on navy. Where it breaks down: (a) light-theme blocks dropped in whole (the cream "Happening now" card, the light-copper cue band, the white Play frame, the cream-bordered box on Starters); (b) too few surface steps, so overlays (toast, next-step panel) and nested cards don't separate from what's under them; (c) the cream fill does three jobs, so nothing reads as *the* action. The two layout breaks (the fold header and the Play frame at 375) aren't Night-specific, but they show up in this packet.

## The five that matter most
1. **D1 (Break).** On Three levels at 375, the fold header with its copy buttons doesn't reflow. In "Our script: the Drive Inventory", "Copy the script" covers the "Read the script" label and "Copy for my AI" runs out of the card to the screen edge. "Read the prompt" breaks to one word per line.
2. **D2 (Break).** At 375 the Play window cuts off the right side of the starter: the Now card, the progress bar, and the agenda rows. This happens in all three Play captures.
3. **D4 (Friction).** On Starters, the eight starters (the page's reason to exist) begin about 3,000px down at 375, about 3.7 screens. At 1280 none shows in the first screen. Four step cards, an audience box, and a 1,300px "Where do I paste it" block come first.
4. **D7 (Friction).** In Night, overlays don't lift off the page. The toast uses the same slate as the remix rows it sits on, so the cut-off row text looks like a rendering glitch. The next-step panel has the card's own fill, no scrim, and covers the button that opened it. The run toast covers "Run my changes" and "Start over".
5. **D6 (Friction).** The cream fill means "current page" (nav pill), "primary action" (Start here, Play), and "step number" (every badge). The badges are the brightest marks in each card and look like buttons, so the one real primary in a view doesn't win.

## Region by region
- **Header (all pages):** a darker navy band with the wordmark and a pill nav. The current page is a cream pill, which is good at 1280, where "Present" is a distinct outlined pill. At 375 the row is clipped and hides Present (D14). The cream pill is one of three uses of cream fill (D6).
- **Now bar (before, during):** a darker band with a light-copper pill (SOON/NOW). It's sticky, which is right for run day. The wrap at 375 is ragged (D28). The link underline is cream, not copper (D13).
- **Part-changed cue:** a full-bleed light-copper band with a navy primary and an outline "Got it". It's legible and the right weight for an interruption, but it contradicts the now bar and card in the capture (D3) and stacks with two other copper elements (D8).
- **Home hero (after):** a strong H1 and a clear cream primary. The lede is too long for a hero at 375 (D9). The buttons wrap unevenly (D26). At 1280 the right half is empty (D25).
- **Home hero (before):** a good date line in Montserrat and a quiet italic note. There's no primary (D19).
- **Happening now card (during):** a cream inverted card, the one place Night goes light (D8).
- **Partner credit (hero and footer):** fixed. On navy, the partner's white hexagon reads clearly. The tagline sits in Montserrat at body size, which is fine as a credit.
- **Copper stripe:** the triple copper band between hero and content. It's the most saturated element in the view, so it works as a divider only if nothing else copper competes nearby (D8). I'd keep it.
- **"On your own? Start here" step cards:** clear three-step pattern. The badges compete (D6), and the CTAs are the copper style while "Everything here" uses "Open" (D13).
- **"Everything here" cards:** the summaries are good, but the "Open" label and its styling fail (D13). The gap above the section is oversized (D22).
- **"Run it with your own team" and "Show one person":** the first is a 21-line block (D9). Both sit on the footer band (D16). The "Show one person" message box with Copy is well built.
- **Footer:** the signature row is clean and correct. The notices run together (D31). The links are a different style from body links (D13).
