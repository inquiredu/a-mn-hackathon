Forming teams of four, by level

  1. During Welcome ({welcome.length}), main room. In the chat, everyone rates how much they've built with AI, 1 to 5, and builders say what they made. The co-host notes the 4s, the 5s, and the builders: the anchors, where they fall.
  2. During Three levels ({levels.length}). The presenter shows the three levels live. Nobody is sorted; the levels are for choosing.
  3. During Show & Tell ({show.length}). Everyone opens the Teams doc and adds their name under the level they want to try, with a partner's name beside it if they have one. The co-host opens Breakout rooms and makes rooms of up to four from each level's list, partners together, an anchor in each room where one fits, then writes the room numbers in the Room column.
  4. When the Remix Arcade starts. Open the rooms. Everyone finds their room number next to their name. Level 1 rooms start with a starter; level 2 and 3 rooms start with their level's example.
  5. Latecomers. Ask which level, add them to that level's smallest room, and write the room number next to their name.

Before, during, and after

Before {weekday}

  • Share the Teams doc so anyone with the link can edit. {Weekday} morning is a good time.
  • Paste a starter into Gemini on a district account (choose Canvas first), and check that it runs. ChatGPT now previews code with a Preview switch instead of Canvas.
  • Set up the three Chrome tabs for Three levels: the Oracle in a chat; the charter with the infographic prompt in Gemini (Canvas on) and the Google Site open to edit; the Apps Script editor with the Drive Inventory, the folder's ID in, run once so the permission screen is behind you.
  • Name the co-host who will set up the breakout rooms.
  • Rehearse with the stage in one window and the speaker notes in another.

In the rooms

  • The anchor shares their screen for the first {buildAlong}. Everyone builds along, then builds their own.
  • Breakout chats start empty: paste the page link when you visit a room.
  • People press Ask for help in Meet to call a host.
  • With {nudge} left: pin something to the gallery wall, even if it's half-built.

Safe play

  • Made-up names and made-up data only. Nothing about real students goes into an AI, and initials or ID numbers don't make data anonymous.
  • Names typed into a running tool stay on that computer. Names pasted into an AI chat go to that company.
  • Public events only in the Calendar Explorer.
  • A published Claude artifact or a public Gemini link is open to anyone who has it. Share inside your district's settings, and never with real information in the tool.
  • Before anyone shares a tool beyond their team, check it with a keyboard, for contrast, and for labels.

The sources behind these: student data, security, and accessibility. For leaders: a five-source reading path.

Show & Cheer and after

  • Builders share with Present now, then A tab, so their tab's sound comes through.
  • If sharing fails, share the gallery wall and click their picture to open what they made.
  • People keep their own work. We don't collect it.
  • The follow-up points to this site, its Sources page, and next month's session on assessment.
