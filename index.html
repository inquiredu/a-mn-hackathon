First Builds

Small tools you can build with AI in twenty minutes to an afternoon, for people who run classrooms, schools, and meetings: a meeting timer, a group maker, a page for your Google Site, a script (a few instructions Google runs for you inside your Drive, after you approve them) that tidies a folder. You copy and paste and ask for changes; the AI writes the code. Everything here works on your own, any day, and is free to use and to change.

On your own? Start here

Twenty minutes, a laptop or Chromebook, and an AI chat you can sign in to: Gemini with your school Google account, ChatGPT, or Claude. Open it in another tab first. Use made-up names and made-up data in everything you try.

  1. 1Play a starterA starter is a small tool that already works: a meeting timer, a group maker, a feedback builder. Press Play and try it.Open the starters
  2. 2Hand it to your AIEach starter has a Copy for my AI button. Press it, paste into your chat, and send. In a few seconds the same tool is running in a panel beside the conversation, and now it's yours to change. Only code, or nothing pasted? The starters page says what to do.Where to paste it
  3. 3Ask for one change"Use our school colors." "Add a sound at the end." Then another. When it breaks, tell the AI exactly what you see. Still broken after two tries? Start a new chat and paste the starter again; that counts.Then invent your own

    Run it with your own team

    The whole two-hour session is here: the run of show, a full-screen slideshow with its own timers to share in your video call (Present, in the menu), speaker notes that follow it from a second window, and chat messages ready to paste. Run it from this site as it is, on any day: the slideshow moves when you press next, and you post your own Meet link and your own copies of two Google files, the Breakout Planner and the walls deck, in the chat. What you give up: on a day other than the first run's, this home page shows your group the kit, not your schedule, so your chat says what's next. The hosts' guide has all of it, written for people who don't code, with the co-host's part spelled out. Optional, for someone comfortable with GitHub: copy the kit and change one text file of times and links, and the clock, the links, and the names on every page become yours, at your own web address.
