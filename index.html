    For anyone who runs a meeting

    The Meeting Timer

    Your agenda on screen with a big countdown, a gentle chime when it's time to move on, and a parking lot.

    Monday: run your next PLC with it on the screen.

    Remix menu
    Make it yours
    • Change the agenda to my meeting: ___ (10 minutes), ___ (15 minutes), ___ (5 minutes).
    • Show our team norms along the bottom: ___.
    • Use our school colors: ___ and ___.
    Make it useful
    • Add a protocol mode: 5 minutes of silent writing, then 10 minutes of discussion, with a different chime for each.
    • Add a spinner that picks who takes notes and who keeps time.
    • Add a button that copies the parking lot as a list I can paste into our notes.
    Make it fun
    • When time runs out, play a game-show buzzer instead of the chime.
    • If we finish the agenda early, celebrate with confetti.
    Look inside (no AI needed)
    • Press Play, then Show the code. Find AGENDA, change the minutes for one item, and press Run my changes.

    For teachers, coaches, and anyone leading a workshop

    The Group Maker

    Paste names, pick a size, and shuffle. It spreads your helpers out and keeps apart anyone who shouldn't be together.

    Monday: tomorrow's lab groups, made in a second.

    Remix menu
    Make it yours
    • Use these group names instead: ___.
    • Make the group cards big enough to read on a projector from the back of the room.
    Make it useful
    • Add a box where I can type pairs who should be kept apart.
    • Give each group roles: facilitator, recorder, timekeeper, and reporter.

    • About names: names typed into the running tool stay on your computer. Names pasted into an AI chat go to that company. Today, stick with the made-up ones.

    Where do I paste it?

    Gemini

    Open gemini.google.com. Turn on Canvas in the prompt bar (it may be under Tools), then paste.

    Claude

    Open claude.ai and paste. The machine appears beside the chat as an artifact.

    ChatGPT

    Open chatgpt.com, choose Canvas from the tools, paste, then press Preview.

    Grant a wish

    Now build something from nothing. It's the same loop you just used, minus the training wheels. It starts with you, the AI does the heavy lifting, and it comes back to you.

    1. 1

      Say what you wish for

      One or two sentences. Who is it for, and what should it do? Fill in the blanks:

      Build me a small web app: [what it is] for [who it's for]. It should [what it does]. Make it simple, colorful, and fun to use. Use made-up data.

    2. 2

      Hand it to your AI

      Paste it into Gemini with Canvas on, Claude, or ChatGPT with Canvas. A first version usually shows up in under a minute.

    3. 3

      Make it yours

      Ask for one change at a time. "Make the buttons bigger." "Add a sound when I win." "Use our school colors."

    4. 4

      Break it, fix it

      Something will go sideways. Tell the AI exactly what you see: "When I press Start, nothing happens."

    5. 5

      Peek under the hood

      Explain three lines of this code to me like I've never coded before. Pick the three most interesting ones.

    6. 6

      Back to you

      Is it what you wished for? Who would you show it to? Pin it to the wall.

    No wish yet? Take one from the jar.

    Choose one to copy it, then paste it into your AI.

    Stuck? Three moves.

    • Describe it. Tell the AI exactly what you see and what you expected instead.
    • Rewind. "Go back to the version before that one."
    • Ask for help. In your breakout room, press Meet's Ask for help button. A host gets the message and will pop into your room.

    Show & Cheer

    A few builders share their screens for two or three minutes each. Applause first, emoji welcome. Then one question for each builder: who would you show this to?

    • 🗓️Most Likely to Actually Get UsedFor the one somebody will really open next week.
