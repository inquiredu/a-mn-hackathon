How-tos that take the mystery out of building with AI, cautions worth knowing, and what research says about learning to code. Every source here was opened and checked on October 7 or 8, 2026.

The short version

Learning to code reliably builds coding skill, and a large meta-analysis found moderate links to creative thinking, math, and metacognition, though those links shrink when compared with other active teaching. The research is stronger on how to begin: run something that works, change it, then make your own. Early studies of AI and beginners suggest AI help need not hurt learning when people still change code by hand and read what the AI made. The main risks are over-reliance, confident mistakes, and data going places it shouldn't, and each has a simple habit that answers it.

How {weekday} is built on this

The morning follows a path researchers call Use-Modify-Create (Lee and colleagues, 2011), later developed into PRIMM: predict, run, investigate, modify, make (Sentance and colleagues, 2019). You start with a starter that works, guess what it does, run it, look inside, change it by hand, remix it with AI, and then build your own wish. Brennan and Resnick (2012) name "reusing and remixing" as a core practice of computational thinking, and their suggestions for assessing it lead straight into next month's conversation.
