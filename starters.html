The Remix Arcade

Starters

Eight small tools that already work, each one useful on Monday. Four run inside your AI chat, two are pages for your Google Site, and two are scripts that do a job in your Google Drive (the three levels, with a starter or two for each). Play one, copy it into your AI chat, and ask for a change.

On your own? You need only this page and an AI chat you can sign in to: Gemini with your school Google account, ChatGPT, or Claude. Open it in another tab. In a live session? Your host links the Breakout Planner in the chat; your room number is next to your name. Running it with your own group? Hosts has what to check before the day and what each part asks of this page.

  1. 1Guess, then play. Pick a starter. Before you press Play, guess what it will do. Then try it.
  2. 2Hand it to your AI. Press Copy for my AI: it copies the tool's whole code with a note asking the AI to run it. In Gemini, choose Canvas first; then paste into your chat and send. In a few seconds the same tool is running beside the chat. Where, in each tool: just below.
  3. 3Remix it. Open the Remix menu, press Copy beside a change, paste it in the same chat, replace each ___ with your own words, and send.
  4. 4Keep it. Changes you make in the Play window vanish when you close it. Your AI chat keeps the version you built there: give the chat a name you'll find on Monday. To hand it to someone, copy a link to your work.

Level 2 · On your site

Pages, not tools: each one ends up on a Google Site you can edit (no Site yet? sites.google.com, then Blank site). Play it, then: 1. Press Copy for my AI and paste into your AI chat (where, above). 2. Copy a remix line such as "Replace the charter with this document," paste it, and paste your document's text where the ___ is. 3. When it looks right, copy the finished code out (each tool's spot is listed above). 4. In your Site: Insert, Embed, the Embed code tab, paste, Next, Insert. 5. Drag the box until the whole page shows. Before you publish, read the page against your document; the AI keeps the meaning, and you check every line. You can also put a starter on a Site as it is: press Play, then Show the code, select it all, copy, and paste it into the Embed code tab. The full steps, and what to do when Sites refuses it: Three levels, level 2.

What Google Sites does to pasted code

Google Sites shows it inside a frame on your page. That keeps your site safe, and it sets the limits: the page can't remember anything between visits, so saved settings and scores vanish; it can't reach the rest of your site or your visitors' accounts; sound and full screen wait for a tap; and a very long page may be refused or load slowly. It shows only as big as you drag the frame. Cut off, or misbehaving? Copy the request below into your AI.

If what you pasted doesn't fit or doesn't behave, hand your AI this:

Make this one self-contained HTML file for a Google Sites embed box about 800 pixels wide: no outside libraries, fonts, or images; nothing saved between visits; no sound until a tap; large type that works on a phone; and as short as it can be while keeping everything it does.

Level 3 · Behind the scenes: scripts, not pages

Nothing to play here. A script is a few instructions Google runs for you inside your Drive, in a tool called Apps Script that's built into every Google account; you approve what it may touch before it runs. Each card has the whole path: what to make first, every step with its place, what you'll see when it worked, and what to do when it breaks. Copy the script copies the script alone, for script.google.com. Copy for my AI copies it with a note asking your AI to explain it line by line before you run it; when your AI hands back a changed script, paste the whole thing over the old one in your project, save, and run again. A remix line or an error message only makes sense to your AI once it has the script, so Copy for my AI comes first. The cautions, on Three levels.
