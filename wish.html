Grant a wish

Describe a small tool in three short answers, and this page turns them into a request you paste into Gemini, ChatGPT, or Claude. In a few minutes you'll have something you can click. No coding, and nothing to install: it starts with you, the AI does the heavy lifting, and it comes back to you. New to this? Start here. (Building a page for your Google Site or a script for your Drive instead? Those levels' prompts and steps work for a wish too: a different document, a different folder, a different job.)

  1. 1

    Say what you wish for

    Fill in three short answers. The wish writes itself. No wish yet? Take one from the jar.

    Your wish, ready to paste:

  2. 2

    Hand it to your AI

    Paste it into your AI chat and send: in Gemini, choose Canvas before you paste; in ChatGPT, press Preview when the code appears; in Claude, just paste. A first version usually shows up in under a minute, as a tool with buttons in a panel beside the chat. Only code and no tool? You skipped Canvas or Preview. Where each switch is, and what you'll see.

  3. 3

    Make it yours

    Ask for one change at a time. "Make the buttons bigger." "Add a sound when I win." "Use our school colors."

  4. 4

    Break it, fix it

    Something will go sideways. Tell the AI exactly what you see: "When I press Start, nothing happens."

  5. 5

    Peek under the hood

    Ask the AI to show you how it works. Paste this into the same chat:

    Explain three lines of this code to me like I've never coded before. Pick the three most interesting ones.

  6. 6

    Back to you

    Is it what you wished for? Who would you show it to? Keep it, and show one person.

Where do I paste it, and what you'll see

Stuck? Three moves.

  • Describe it. Tell the AI exactly what you see and what you expected instead.
  • Rewind. "Go back to the version before that one."
  • Ask for help. On your own, ask the AI itself: "I'm a teacher who has never coded. Here is what I see: … What should I do?" Still stuck after two tries? Start a new chat and paste your wish again; that counts. In a live session, press Ask for help in your Meet breakout room and a host will join you.

Keep it, and show one person

Got something working, even halfway? Half-built counts. Copy a link to it so you can open it Monday, take a picture of it, and show one person: paste the picture and the link into an email or a Doc, or just turn your screen toward a colleague. In a live session, the picture goes on the gallery wall your host links in the chat, where everyone can click it to open what you made.

1. Copy a link to your work

Gemini

At the top right of the canvas, choose Share & export, then Share, then Copy link.

Claude

Click the tool Claude made (the panel beside the chat; Claude calls it an artifact) and choose Publish, then copy the link. On a school or work plan, choose Share, then Share & copy link.

ChatGPT

At the top of the chat, choose Share, then Create link or Copy link. The link opens the conversation, code and all.

Microsoft Copilot

On your page, choose Share at the top right, then Copy link.

A link from a school account may open only for people in your district. The picture in step 2 shows your work to everyone either way. Each tool's own help on sharing.

2. Take a picture of it

Windows

Press Windows + Shift + S and drag a box around your work. It's copied, ready to paste.

Mac

Press Command + Control + Shift + 4 and drag a box around your work. It's copied, ready to paste.

Chromebook

Press Ctrl + Shift + Show windows (the key with a rectangle and two lines, above the 6), drag a box around your work, and choose Capture. It's copied, ready to paste.

3. In a live session: put it on the wall

  1. Open the gallery wall from your host's link in the chat: a Google Slides deck with a few empty slides for pictures. Click a slide with room on it; when one fills up, use the next.
  2. Paste: Ctrl + V, or Command + V on a Mac.
  3. Drag a corner to make it postcard-size, and move it to an open spot.
  4. With the picture still selected, press Ctrl + K (Command + K on a Mac), paste your link, and choose Apply.

Now anyone can click your picture to open what you made. A wall shared with a whole group is open to everyone in it, and so is a published link, so keep to made-up data. If the wall opens view-only on your school account, your district may block outside files: paste your picture in the Meet chat instead.
